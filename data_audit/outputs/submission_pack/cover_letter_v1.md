# Cover Letter — Nature Communications

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "State-dependent NAMPT-axis transcriptional programs in obesity and exercise," for consideration as an Article in Nature Communications.

**The question.** NAMPT-related biology is conventionally read as a single directional marker ("NAMPT is good" in exercise, "NAMPT is inflammatory" in obesity). We show that this reading collapses when the axis is measured as a two-program transcriptional state (repair-like NAD-salvage/sirtuin/mitochondrial programs versus inflammation-like NF-κB/innate-immune programs) across tissues, time points and metabolic backgrounds.

**What we did.** We built a 59-gene, two-program NAMPT-axis score and applied it to nine public human transcriptomic units (337 samples; 8 GEO accessions) spanning exercise, obesity/weight loss and a human muscle-cell model, integrating evidence by pre-specified contrasts, random-effects meta-analysis, module-level internal consistency and sensitivity analysis. We further used the Evo2-40B genomic foundation model to prioritize 432 regulatory variants and stratify them against GTEx eQTL and GWAS Catalog evidence, reporting honestly that the module yields no Tier A/B overlap and is therefore presented as supplementary, with a Tier C hypothesis list for future MPRA/CRISPRi validation.

**What is new.** (1) A reproducible transcriptomic evidence ladder showing that acute exercise raises NAMPT without a reproducible repair-program gain, while lipotoxic stimulation couples the same upregulation to a stronger inflammatory program; (2) a state-dependence framework that explains part of the apparent contradiction in the eNAMPT/NAD literature; (3) strict claim boundaries: all results are transcriptomic, eNAMPT protein/NAD-metabolite directionality is a testable prediction, and no causal or clinical claims are made.

**Suitability for Nature Communications.** The study is a fully reproducible, public-data, secondary-analysis contribution — no new wet experiments — that reframes a widely used single-marker biomarker as a context-dependent transcriptional state, with a clear and honest validation path (protein/metabolite measurements, prospective exercise cohorts, targeted variant experiments). We believe this fits the journal's interest in reproducible, boundary-respecting quantitative biology.

All authors approved the manuscript and declare no competing interests. This work has not been submitted elsewhere.
